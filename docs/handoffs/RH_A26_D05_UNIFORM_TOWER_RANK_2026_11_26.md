## 1. Fixed-field rank and the actual dual module

Write l for the measuring prime, G for the absolute Galois group in a common algebraic closure, chi_l for the cyclotomic character, V for a closed subgroup, H=V intersect ker chi_l. The actual XDual(N,H,F_l,F_l) consists of locally constant additive characters, zero on H intersect all inertia groups, and additionally on H intersect decomposition groups above divisors of N. For N=1 the extra condition is vacuous. XRk(l,N,H,r) asserts finiteness and cardinal <=l^r. CharOff(V,ineOff(N),l) is a larger space: it only requires inertia vanishing away from primes dividing N. These are distinct conditions. In particular XDual(1)=CharOff with N=1 and XDual(N) is a subspace of XDual(1).

For a fixed abelian number field K with HasMuQ, the exact Published.FerreroWashington input gives one r bounding |Cl(K(mu_(l^n)))/l Cl|<=l^r at every finite level, including every choice of cyclotomic field representative. Its usual classical origin is mu=0 in the cyclotomic Z_l extension of an abelian number field: the structure theorem for its class-group Iwasawa module and finite-layer control yield bounded mod-l ranks. We use the actual stated finite-layer proposition as an input, not a new proof of Ferrero-Washington or a claim of formal equivalence of the classical Iwasawa modules. At n>=2 the finite layer is totally imaginary (it contains a root of order >2), so ordinary Hilbert class field reciprocity identifies everywhere-unramified finite additive characters with Hom(Cl,F_l). Its size equals |Cl/lCl| by finite abelian group decomposition. The source levelRk only needs surjectivity of this identification onto XDual(1); local compatibility is the usual inertia condition, not an assumption of all primes splitting.

To verify infRk, let V=G_K and H=G_{K(mu_(l^infty))}; H_n=G_{K(mu_(l^n))} decreases to H. For a continuous additive character f:H->F_l, choose an open normal W in V such that H intersect W is contained in ker f. The character extends to HW by f(hw)=f(h), well-defined via HW/W. Compactness gives H_n subset HW eventually. If f vanishes on H intersect every inertia group, the extended character vanishes on H_n intersect inertia at every place for large n: away from l these intersections equal H intersect inertia since l-power cyclotomic extensions are unramified there; above l the union of inertia groups of the finitely many primes of K over l is compact (each is a conjugacy union of a closed subgroup). The closed nonzero locus of the extension in that compact union misses H, hence misses sufficiently deep H_n. This is the N=1 instance of the source xdualExt, also in #610/#614. Restrictions of finite-level characters form increasing images in XDual(1,H) because H_n is decreasing, and exhaust that space. Each image has at most l^r elements. Any l^r+1 distinct elements would lie in a common image, impossible. This proves fwRk. For AbF A, its fixing subgroup abU is exactly the preimage of A.H under the finite cyclotomic character at A.m; its fixed field is abelian of degree A.deg and embeds at A.m. Every representative has the same embedded image up to algebraic-closure transport, by the fixing criterion and commutativity of the cyclotomic group. Thus the same bound applies in any algebraic closure, proving abRk using actual transport, not a choice of r for every representative.

For the actual Dinf(l,N,V), all continuous characters into Q_l/Z_l have finite image by compactness; the module is l-primary torsion. Its first torsion layer maps injectively to XDual(N,H,F_l,F_l) via the canonical identification (Q_l/Z_l)[l]=F_l, with both local conditions preserved. Hence dimension of Dinf[l] is <=r. To see dualGen directly, choose an injection of Dinf[l] into (Q_l/Z_l)^r[l] and extend its r coordinates to Dinf by injectivity of the divisible Q_l/Z_l over the PID Z_l. The resulting map Dinf -> (Q_l/Z_l)^r is injective: a nonzero element in its kernel would have a nonzero multiple killed by l. Every linear map Dinf->Q_l/Z_l extends across this embedding. The endomorphism ring of Q_l/Z_l over Z_l is Z_l, since restrictions to its cyclic torsion layers are multiplications by compatible residues. Thus the r coordinate maps generate the full algebraic dual, exactly the source GenLE (surjection from Z_l^r). This proves genOfRk for both N=1 and N=l, since XDual(N) is a subspace of XDual(1). Together with root and complexity conditions, towerFixed holds with R=max(1,r), exactly as in the source.

## 2. The two prime congruences and place orbits

Let M be the source AbF, D=M.deg>0, m=M.m. Its fixed number field is K. withI M corresponds to K(i), and base2 M q d corresponds to K(i) composed with the two quadratic subfields of Q(mu_q), Q(mu_d). This follows from the source sup construction (intersection of pulled-back fixing subgroups). For odd prime p the square subgroup of (Z/p)^* has index 2, giving the unique quadratic subfield. No identification of its discriminant is needed. Define V=cycU(2,abU(withI M))=G_K intersect ker chi_2; adjoining i is redundant after imposing the full cyclotomic kernel. V is normal and closed in G_Q. The source PrimePair hypotheses give q≡3 mod 8, d≡7 mod 16, prime, and coprimality to m. For p=q,d, inertia at p fixes all roots of unity of orders prime to p, hence belongs to V because p is coprime to 2m. This proves ineTwo.

At an absolute place over odd p the decomposition group maps under chi_2 to a closed subgroup of Z_2^* containing p, by the unramified action of arithmetic Frobenius on all 2-power roots. If p≡3 mod 8 then v_2(p^2-1)=3 and p≠1 mod 8; if p≡7 mod 16 then v_2(p^2-1)=4 and p≠1 mod 16. For a=3 or 4, p^2 generates densely 1+2^a Z_2 by the logarithm or elementary valuation growth (the identity verified in #614). The closed subgroup containing p thus contains that principal-unit subgroup and has at least two cosets of it. As [Z_2^*:1+2^a Z_2]=2^(a-1), its index is at most 2^(a-2), i.e. 2 or 4. This proves idxAdic and decIndex without assuming density of Frobenius in all of Z_2^*.

The absolute places over p are a transitive G_Q-set with stabilizer a decomposition group D_p. The V-orbits correspond to double cosets V\G_Q/D_p, equivalently cosets of VD_p because V normal. With H=ker chi_2, W=G_K and V=H intersect W, the index [HD_p:VD_p] is <=[H:V]<=[G_Q:W]=D: coset representatives of H modulo V suffice for HD_p modulo VD_p. Thus [G_Q:VD_p]<=[G_Q:HD_p] D. This gives at most 2D or 4D orbits, respectively, exactly orbTwo. All facts about decomposition, Frobenius, and inertia here use the conventional absolute-local place identifications and local cyclotomic unramifiedness.

## 3. Adding ramification and then passing to quadratic subgroups

For fixed V and allowed ramification N, define C_N=CharOff(V,ineOff(N),l). Suppose p is a rational prime different from l, its absolute inertia groups lie in V, and there are at most k V-orbits of places above p. Evaluation of a character in C_(Np) at one chosen tame generator for each orbit defines a linear map to F_l^k (padding unused coordinates if needed). Classical local inertia structure gives that the wild inertia is pro-p and tame quotient procyclic away from p, so a continuous homomorphism to F_l is determined by one tame-generator value. This is exactly the tameGen conclusion used here, with its classical input explicit. A homomorphism is invariant under conjugation by elements of its own group V. Consequently the kernel of these evaluations is precisely C_N: vanishing on each representative inertia implies vanishing on all its conjugates, while other forbidden inertia were already zero. Therefore |C_(Np)|<=|C_N| l^k, with finiteness as part of the conclusion. This proves charStep by the same fiber argument as the source, no independence of the evaluations assumed.

At l=2 apply this twice, still on the FIXED subgroup V. From the fixed-field FerreroWashington bound |C_1|<=2^g for K(i), obtain
    |C_q|<=2^(g+2D),
    |C_(qd)|<=2^(g+6D).
The order matters: the orbit bound was established for V, not for the subsequently enlarged fields.

For the next two steps, consider U normal open of index 2 in V' (or index 1), and S the forbidden inertia set, invariant under V' conjugation, with S intersect V' subset U. Let C_U, C_V' be the respective character spaces into F_2 vanishing on S. Choose sigma outside U. Conjugation induces involution T on C_U since sigma^2 lies in U and inner conjugation acts trivially. In characteristic 2, (T-I)^2=0. Its image lies in its kernel C_U^T, so dim C_U<=2 dim C_U^T, provided the latter finite (kernel/image dimension argument proves finiteness too).

To bound invariants, restriction C_V'->C_U^T has kernel exactly the one-dimensional sign character V'/U, allowed because forbidden elements lie in U. For an invariant character phi on U, extension to V' exists exactly if phi(sigma^2)=0: necessity follows from 2=0; sufficiency by defining phi(u sigma^j)=phi(u) for j=0,1, and checking multiplication using invariance and phi(sigma^2)=0. Continuity follows from the open cosets; forbidden values remain zero. The extendable subspace thus has codimension at most one inside invariants. Hence dim C_U^T<=dim C_V'. This reconstructs invTwo and idxTwo, including the possible obstruction to extending a character. For index 1 the bound is immediate. No semisimplicity at 2.

Apply to each of the two quadratic fixing subgroups successively, keeping N=qd. Every forbidden inertia element fixes the quadratic field of conductor p dividing N, since the field is unramified outside p. Thus S intersect V' subset U, and the index divides 2. This proves quadStep and gives
    |C_final| <= (2^(g+6D))^4 = 2^(4(g+6D)).
The actual XDual(1,cycU(2,abU(base2 M q d))) is a subspace of C_final, hence has this same upper bound. This proves baseRk. Taking R=4(g+6D), which is positive since D>0, and using section 1's dual generation for N=1,2, proves towerTwo with the exact source constant, before q,d vary.

## 4. Actual base and composite-field transfer

The field L for base2 M q d contains i, so is totally complex; by successive degree bounds [L:Q]<=8D and it embeds at 4mqd. These are the degree and embedding requirements of TwoBase. For h>=2 put L'=L(mu_(2^(h+2))). Its relative degree is a power 2^j with j<=h+2 (it is a subgroup order of the cyclotomic unit group and contains i). This is layerFn. In the same algebraic closure compare E with the source's actual IsCompField fixing criterion. The absolute fixing group of L' requires cyclotomic exponent 1 modulo 2^(h+2), original M kernel condition, and both quadratic conditions; the mod-4 condition is redundant. Normality of the fields makes the comparison independent of the embeddings chosen in the common closure.

For the actual first locally trivial cohomology with Q=Q_2/Z_2 and twist chi_2^(-e), restriction to G_L' preserves local vanishing by the same inertia/decomposition intersections as #594/#609. Its kernel on full H^1 is H^1(G_E/G_L',Q^{G_L'}) by the low-degree inflation-restriction argument: subtract a global coboundary to make restriction zero and descend. The index-one case gives zero. For C2, let sigma act on B=Q^{G_L'}; the group H^1 is ker(1+sigma)/im(sigma-1). It is killed by 2 since on the numerator (sigma-1)b=-2b. B is a submodule of Q and hence either finite cyclic 2-primary or Q itself; any subquotient of B is again finite cyclic or Q (every proper subgroup of Q has bounded order). A subquotient killed by 2 has length at most 1. This proves kerTwo, not assuming restriction injective at 2. The kernel on the locally trivial submodule has length at most 1 and its image is inside the local kernel upstairs. Additivity of extended lengths gives shaInv(E,2,e)<=1+shaInv(L',2,e), exactly shaTwo, by the source arithmetic-model identifications already audited.
