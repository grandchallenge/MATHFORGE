## 1. Actual coefficients and finite input

Fix p=l, R=Z_p, a number field F, its algebraic closure and actual LData D. Let G=G_S for S exactly the finite nonempty set of primes above p, and G_v the specified decomposition groups with localization maps to G. The cyclotomic character chi is D.chi on G and agrees on the absolute group and on the local groups via D.hchi and the chosen places. Fix integer e, T=R(e+1) with action chi^(e+1), A=Q_p/R(-e) with action chi^(-e). All cochains here are continuous with the source coefficient topologies. Set T_r=T/p^r T, A_r=A[p^r]. Evaluation at 1 identifies the source TDual(T_r), twisted by chi, with R/p^r acting via chi*chi^(-(e+1))=chi^(-e). The map a mod p^r -> a/p^r mod R identifies this module with A_r equivariantly. Reduction on T is ordinary reduction, inclusion A_r->A_s for s>=r is multiplication by p^(s-r) in these coordinates. In particular a finite coefficient dual is NOT reduced by the same map as T.

Under PTCase (p odd or F totally complex), apply the exact finite-level PoitouTateDuality proposition to T_r. It supplies NineTermExact, ShaDual, H^3(T_1)=0, at every positive level. The local-field identification is the usual decomposition group of an algebraic extension with the absolute group of the completion, respecting the action on roots of unity; the field F_v is finite over Q_p. We retain this standard identification and its formal correspondence as inputs. LocalFinitenessK gives finite H^1,H^2(T_r) locally and H^3(T_1)=0. ShaDual gives finiteness of both global local kernels in degrees 1,2; since the product of local groups is finite, global H^1,H^2(T_r) are finite too. Finite local duality identifies the cardinality of H^2(G_v,T_r) with that of H^0(G_v,A_r). NineTermExact identifies the cokernel of global-to-local H^2(T_r) with the finite dual of H^0(G,A_r), hence equal cardinality. ShaDual gives equality of the cardinalities of the finite local kernels K_r=Sha^2(T_r) and S_r=Sha^1(A_r). Here a finite R/p^r-module has a dual of equal size: use cyclic decomposition and Hom(R/p^a,R/p^r)=p^(r-a)R/p^r. No compatibility of the chosen finite perfect pairings or local invariant generators is required for the source's length-only proof.

## 2. Reduction and finite generation of actual continuous H^2

For each group G or G_v, let X=H^2(T), X_r=H^2(T_r). Reduction on continuous cochains commutes with differential and restriction. Any continuous cochain into a finite quotient has a continuous lift to the next quotient or to T by choosing representatives (the coefficient target is discrete and finite). Multiplication by p^r on T is injective with continuous inverse on its image. If an integral 2-cocycle reduces to a coboundary, lift its continuous primitive, subtract its differential, then divide by p^r; the resulting continuous 2-cochain is a cocycle. Thus the reduction kernel is p^r X, exactly reduction_h2_eq_zero_iff.

To lift a finite 2-cocycle mod p^r, lift it as a 2-cochain mod p^(r+1); its differential is a 3-cocycle in p^r T/p^(r+1)T=T_1. H^3(T_1)=0 gives a continuous 2-cochain primitive for that differential; subtract its p^r-multiple (identified by the injection T_1) to obtain a cocycle lifting the original cochain. Repeat, and take the compatible coefficient limit. Continuity follows from continuity of every finite reduction; cocycle identity holds since intersection p^r T=0. This proves surjectivity, not merely surjectivity on a chosen finite subset.

Separatedness is essential. Suppose a continuous 2-cocycle z is a coboundary at every finite level. At level r consider the nonempty set of its continuous primitives modulo 1-coboundaries, a torsor under finite H^1(T_r). Reduction gives maps between these finite nonempty sets. Their inverse limit is nonempty by compactness of the product and the finite intersection property (each finite list can be lifted from its highest level). Choose compatible primitive classes. Starting at level 1, choose representatives recursively: the reduction of a next-level representative differs from the chosen previous primitive by a 1-coboundary; lift its degree-zero primitive and subtract the corresponding coboundary upstairs. This makes the representatives themselves compatible. Their coefficient limit is continuous and has differential z. Hence intersection p^r X=0. This proves the exact instance of padic_coboundary_of_divisible, without supposing that all finite cochain spaces are finite, or that transition maps between primitive sets are surjective.

Finally X/pX finite implies finite generation of X using separation. Choose finitely many lifts x_i of generators of X/pX. Recursively express any x as a linear combination of the x_i modulo p^r X, using successive coefficients in {0,...,p-1}. Each coefficient sequence converges in R, and the difference between x and the resulting finite R-linear combination lies in every p^r X (the coefficient tails are divisible by p^r). Separation makes it zero. This does not assume completeness of X. Thus local_lim/global_lim hold, including finite generation and natural identifications X/p^r X = X_r. This argument works for the actual continuous-cochain cohomology; exact formal correspondence of the source's cochain definitions is retained as a separate obligation.

## 3. Local length equality and global cokernel identity

For any finite-generated R-module M, length M equals the supremum of lengths M/p^r M, by the structure theorem (free part gives unbounded lengths, torsion part stabilizes). For any p-primary torsion module N, length N equals supremum of lengths N[p^r], since any finite strict chain can be witnessed by finitely many elements killed by a common p-power. A finite R-module has length log_p of its cardinality. Invariants commute with taking torsion: H^0(G_v,A)[p^r]=H^0(G_v,A_r). Hence the finite-level local cardinal equalities and section 2 give
    length H^2(G_v,T)=length H^0(G_v,A).
This is local_tate_duality_h2_limit, valid for e=0 too.

Let X=H^2(G,T), P=product_v H^2(G_v,T), f:X->P localization, C=coker f, K=ker f. They are finite-generated over the DVR (including K by Noetherianity). Reduction gives f_r:X/p^r X->P/p^r P commuting with f, because reduction and restriction commute on cochains. Its cokernel is canonically P/(fX+p^r P)=C/p^r C. Thus finite-level NineTermExact and the torsion identification yield |C/p^r C|=|H^0(G,A)[p^r]|, so length C=length H^0(G,A), the exact ptCokerAt. We have not asserted that reduction commutes with kernels.

## 4. Kernel comparison: why the source splits on e=0

In general ker f_r can be larger than K/(K intersect p^r X). The latter maps injectively to ker f_r; surjectivity holds precisely when
    fX intersect p^r P = p^r fX.
Indeed for f(x)=p^r y in the intersection, choose x' with f(x)=p^r f(x'), so x-p^r x' lies in K and represents the same reduction. Conversely such a representative implies the displayed condition.

For e nonzero, the local cyclotomic image at every v over p is infinite: finite local fields cannot contain all p-power roots of unity. Some g has chi(g)^(-e)!=1. Any invariant a in A is killed by that nonzero scalar, hence by p^c_v for a finite c_v. Therefore each local H^0(A) is finite, and by section 3 the corresponding H^2(T) has finite length. Since S finite, a common power p^m annihilates P. For r>=m, p^r P=0 and the intersection condition follows. This is exists_kill and exists_kill_pi in the source. At e=0, A is trivial and H^0(G,A)=A, so the exact finite cokernel cardinal from section 3 is |C/p^r C|=p^r at every r>=1. The DVR structure theorem gives C free of rank one (growth slope one and no constant torsion offset), hence p-torsion-free. If f(x)=p^r y then p^r [y]=0 in C, so y=f(x') for some x'; this proves the same intersection condition at every level. This is why the source cannot simply use the nonzero-twist argument at e=0.

Example of failure without the condition: X=P=Z_p, f multiplication by p. K=0, yet every ker f_r contains a nonzero subgroup of order p. Here C has p-torsion. Finite kernel cardinal alone does not recover the integral kernel.

## 5. The other side: first cohomology with torsion coefficients

Set S_infty=Sha^1(G,A) with the same local maps. Let B_r be the image in S_infty of Sha^1(G,A_r) under coefficient inclusion. These are ascending R-submodules, and their union is S_infty. Indeed choose a continuous cocycle representing a locally trivial class; its image is finite by compactness of G and discreteness of A, hence killed by some p-power. At each of finitely many v choose a local coboundary witness b_v in A; enlarge r to kill all witnesses. Then the cocycle and its local witnesses are already in A_r. No finite-generation assumption on S_infty.

The finite local-kernel map may have a kernel: a finite-level cocycle that is globally a coboundary in A need not be a coboundary in A_r. Suppose z=delta a in A and z|G_v=delta b_v with b_v in A_r. For e nonzero, a-b_v is invariant locally and killed by p^c for a common c as in section 4. S is nonempty (a prime above p exists). Choosing any v, if r>=c, then p^r(a-b_v)=p^r b_v=0, hence a itself is in A_r. Thus z is already a global coboundary in A_r. For e=0 all coboundaries into A are zero and the injection is immediate. This verifies the exact extra condition in sha1_torsion_level_filtration. Consequently at all sufficiently large r, |B_r|=|Sha^1(G,A_r)|. We do not assert B_r=S_infty[p^r]; the actual filtration is by images with local witnesses, not necessarily the full torsion layer.

## 6. Assembly

For every sufficiently large r (e.g. r>=c+m+1 for e nonzero, r>=1 for e=0), sections 4 and 5 and finite ShaDual give
    |K/(K intersect p^r X)| = |ker f_r| = |Sha^1(G,A_r)| = |B_r|.
The submodules K intersect p^r X decrease to zero by separation. For a module with descending submodules N_r of intersection zero, every finite strict chain of submodules can be detected in the quotient only with care: separation alone does not imply arbitrary submodules are closed. In our actual case K and X are finite-generated R-modules; all their submodules are closed in the p-adic topology by the DVR structure theorem (quotient is separated). More directly, Artin-Rees for K subset X gives for some a and r>=a, K intersect p^r X=p^(r-a)(K intersect p^a X) subset p^(r-a)K. Hence the induced filtration is cofinal with the p-adic filtration of K. Therefore supremum of quotient lengths is length K. This also follows by compactness of finite-generated p-adic modules. On the other side B_r exhausts S_infty, so supremum of their lengths is length S_infty by finite chain witnesses. Taking suprema yields length Sha2Zl(D,e)=shaLen(D,e) including infinite lengths, the exact PTShaAt for every e. (For the more general source length lemma, bounded lengths of the quotients force stabilization of the descending N_r and hence N_r=0 eventually; if unbounded both sides are infinite. This is an alternative not requiring Artin-Rees.)
