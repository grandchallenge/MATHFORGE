## Primary source

Jacques Magnen, Vincent Rivasseau, Roland Sénéor, “Construction of YM_4 with an infrared cutoff,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **155** (1993), 325–383, DOI `10.1007/BF02097397`.

The author-uploaded full-body rendering previously admitted as `YM-SRC-025` was re-inspected specifically for the exact relevant-mass-counterterm step.

## Exact MRS statements

### 1. Counterterm list and exact-tuning requirement — Section III, printed p. 348

MRS introduce the independent cutoff-restoring counterterms in their Eq. (III.1), including the relevant quadratic term with coefficient `b_rho` multiplying the normalized `A^2/2` operator.

Immediately after Eq. (III.1), the paper states that this relevant counterterm must be **fine tuned exactly** so that the renormalized mass is zero.

The paper then says that this is the same problem as fixing the critical bare mass in infrared scalar `phi^4_4`, cites `[FMRS1]` and `[R]`, and says the problem **should be solved** either:

- by a fixed-point argument as in `[R]`; or
- by full renormalization of the two-point function together with a one-particle-irreducible analysis as in `[FMRS1]`.

This passage is a requirement and proof-route pointer. It is not an MRS-specific theorem establishing the exact choice of `b_rho`.

### 2. Repetition at the multiscale-flow level — Section V.E, printed p. 366

When discussing preservation of the theory under the scale flow, MRS again distinguish marginal terms, for which one-loop treatment is said to suffice, from the **relevant mass operator**.

For the relevant mass operator they again state that the value of the mass counterterm has to be fixed **exactly by a fixed-point method**, citing the critical mass construction in infrared `phi^4_4` in `[R]`.

The same discussion notes additional gauge-theory structure: the cutoff is not gauge invariant, gauge-restoring counterterms flow at every scale, and the background/homothetic gauge choice is used to simplify wave-function renormalization. Therefore the scalar fixed-point construction cannot be treated as hypothesis-identical by citation alone.

### 3. Section VI uses the quadratic counterterm but does not close the exact tuning theorem

In the background-field stability analysis, MRS state that the `B^2` and `B^4` counterterm contributions are crucial and that their **full value** must be included in the effective potential.

Around Eq. (VI.16), the paper checks normalization by matching the mass term coming from the Faddeev–Popov determinant with the corresponding piece of the mass counterterm computed in Section III.

This is a normalization/stability consistency check. It does not define an exact all-order mass functional `M_{rho,R}(b;lambda)`, does not prove an invariant interval for `b`, and does not state a derivative/Lipschitz/contraction estimate for exact zero-mass tuning.

## Cited scalar template

### FMRS1

J. Feldman, J. Magnen, V. Rivasseau, R. Sénéor, “Construction and Borel summability of infrared phi^4_4 by a phase space expansion,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **109** (1987), 437–480, DOI `10.1007/BF01206146`.

Theorem I.1 states, in the scalar model, that for small coupling one can choose the mass counterterm so that the thermodynamic limit exists and is massless.

More importantly for the proof mechanism, the paper explicitly defines its running mass subtraction inductively: the scale-`i` mass counterterm is built from the negative zero-external-momentum value of sums of one-particle-irreducible two-point subgraphs, with the lower-scale counterterms already known.

That supplies a concrete scalar **proof template**:

`local relevant projection of 1PI two-point sector -> inductive mass subtraction -> exact critical mass choice`.

It does not supply the corresponding Yang–Mills theorem.

## Exact hypothesis mismatch

The FMRS1 theorem and its 1PI subtraction construction concern:

- scalar `phi^4_4`;
- the infrared/thermodynamic limit;
- a fixed ultraviolet cutoff;
- scalar phase-space running couplings and scalar 1PI two-point subgraphs.

MRS MT1 requires:

- pure four-dimensional `SU(2)` gauge fields;
- trivial topological sector;
- fixed infrared cutoff;
- removal of the ultraviolet cutoff;
- regularized axial/background/homothetic gauge structure;
- gauge-restoring counterterms generated by a non-gauge-invariant cutoff;
- compatibility with the MRS background-field/large-field stability architecture.

No theorem or comparison map was located in MRS that transports the scalar FMRS1 critical-mass theorem to this gauge-theory setting.

This negative source-body finding is bounded to the inspected MRS paper and its cited scalar template. It is not a proof that no later literature can supply such a theorem.

A theorem-grade native closure needs an MRS-specific relevant projection of the renormalized 1PI two-point sector and a quantitative response estimate strong enough to solve the exact mass equation.

A sufficient fixed-point interface would have the following shape for fixed infrared regulator `R` and small `lambda`:

1. define an exact relevant self-energy projection `Sigma_{rho,R}^{rel}(b;lambda)`;
2. define the mass equation
   `b = F_{rho,R}(b;lambda)`
   from that relevant projection, with signs/normalizations fixed by the MRS action convention;
3. prove an interval `I_R(lambda)`, independent of sufficiently large `rho`, with
   `F_{rho,R}(I_R) subset I_R`;
4. prove
   `|F_{rho,R}(b)-F_{rho,R}(b')| <= q_R |b-b'|`
   with `q_R < 1`, uniformly in sufficiently large `rho`;
5. prove compatibility of the selected fixed point with the counterterm normalization and background-field stability interface used in Sections V–VI.

An implicit-function version is equally acceptable if it provides the corresponding MRS-specific nondegeneracy and uniformity estimates.
