## Theorem 12.4 — integral rank-one freeness has an odd-prime hypothesis

Kato Theorem 12.4 states for a stable lattice `T`:

1. `H^2(T)` is torsion over the Iwasawa algebra;
2. `H^1(T)` is torsion-free and becomes free rank one after tensoring with `Q`;
3. if `p != 2` and the residual two-dimensional representation is irreducible, then `H^1(T)` is free rank one integrally.

Thus the theorem's integral freeness upgrade in part (3) is not a selected `p=2` theorem.

This does not prevent use of parts (1) and (2) at `p=2`, but those parts alone do not determine the integral height-one `(2)` exponent.

## Theorem 12.5 — rational divisibility versus integral divisibility

Kato Theorem 12.5 constructs the zeta morphism and gives torsion/divisibility information after inverting `p`.

The decisive integral clause is Theorem 12.5(4). Its statement begins by assuming

`p != 2`

and condition `(12.5.2)`, namely a large-image condition requiring the image over the cyclotomic extension to contain `SL_2(Z_p)` in a suitable basis.

Under those hypotheses Kato places the zeta module inside integral `H^1(T)` and obtains the corresponding all-height-one length inequality, apart from the explicitly named local exceptional case `(12.5.1)`.

Therefore Theorem 12.5(4) is not directly available at the selected prime `p=2`.

## Theorem 12.6 — integral Euler-system generators do not close the missing divisibility

Theorem 12.6 defines a concrete integral submodule `Z` generated by Kato's integral Euler-system classes and proves

`Z subset Z(f,T)`

with finite quotient `Z(f,T)/Z`.

This is useful integral structure, but it is not an all-height-one `p=2` main-conjecture divisibility theorem. In particular it does not remove the explicit `p != 2` hypothesis from Theorem 12.5(4), nor does it compute the missing height-one `(2)` exponent.

## Theorem 17.4 — the exact cyclotomic boundary

Assume good ordinary reduction at the chosen prime. Kato defines the ordinary cyclotomic Selmer module `X(T)` and proves:

### Part (1)

`X(T)` is torsion over the Iwasawa algebra.

### Part (2)

For every height-one prime `q` of the Iwasawa algebra **which does not contain `p`**, Kato proves

`length_{Lambda_q}(X(T)_q) <= ord_q(L_p-adic)`.

No `p != 2` hypothesis appears in this part. Hence at `p=2` Kato supplies genuine control away from the height-one prime containing `2`.

### Part (3)

To extend the inequality to **every** height-one prime, Kato explicitly assumes

`p != 2`

and condition `(12.5.2)`, together with integral choices of the period/basis data.

Thus the upgrade that would include the height-one prime `(2)` is precisely the part not available at the selected prime.
