## 1. Primary source

Germán Sierra, *The Riemann zeros as energy levels of a Dirac fermion in a potential built from the prime numbers in Rindler spacetime*, Journal of Physics A: Mathematical and Theoretical 47 (2014) 325204, DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/47/32/325204, arXiv:1404.4252v3.

The source constructs a massless Dirac Hamiltonian in the right Rindler wedge with an infinite array of delta-function mirror interactions. The chiral free evolution is generated by the Berry–Keating dilation operator

\[
\pm\frac{x\hat p+\hat p x}{2}.
\]

The mirrors break scale invariance and are intended to encode arithmetic information.

## 2. Fixed self-adjoint model data

The source derives a self-adjoint Hamiltonian \(H_R\) acting on wavefunctions satisfying:

- one boundary condition at the observer worldline \(\rho=\ell_1\), parameterized by one phase \(e^{i\vartheta}\);
- matching conditions across each mirror \(\rho=\ell_n\);
- a fixed infinite collection of mirror locations and reflection data.

The source summarizes the self-adjoint realization as characterized by

\[
\{\ell_n,r_n,r'_n\}_{n=2}^{\infty}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\vartheta.
\]

For a **single fixed operator**, all of these data, including \(\vartheta\), must be fixed independently of the eigenvalue under test.

This fixed-domain requirement is the controlling RH-001 contract for this audit.

## 3. Independently specified arithmetic mechanism

The source contains a materially useful construction that is not obtained by inserting the known zero ordinates.

For the Riemann model it chooses

\[
\ell_n=n^{1/2},
\qquad
\varrho_n=\varepsilon\,\frac{\mu(n)}{n^\sigma},
\]

where \(\mu(n)\) is the Möbius function.

This has two relevant consequences.

1. The mirror geometry \(\ell_n\propto\sqrt n\) produces primitive classical trajectories associated with primes whose observer-time periods are \(\log p\).
2. The reflection amplitudes use \(\mu(n)\), so the arithmetic input is prime-factorization data rather than a list of zeta zeros.

This is an important positive result for the campaign:

\[
\text{prime-labelled orbit structure can be inserted independently of zero ordinates}.
\]

RH-R033's missing prime mechanism is therefore not a generic impossibility.

## 4. Reciprocal-zeta mechanism and its conditional range

In the weak-reflection analysis, the source obtains formally

\[
e^{-i\Phi_\infty}R_\infty
=
\varepsilon
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{\sigma+iE}}
=
\frac{\varepsilon}{\zeta(\sigma+iE)}.
\]

The source itself qualifies the domain of this identity:

- for \(\sigma\ge 1\), the reciprocal-zeta Dirichlet series is in its unconditional convergence region;
- for \(1/2<\sigma<1\), the displayed use is conditional on RH.

Accordingly, no argument that assumes this reciprocal-zeta representation throughout \(1/2<\sigma<1\) may subsequently be used as an independent proof of RH.

That would cross the noncircularity gate.

## 5. Critical-line construction

The source then studies \(\sigma\to 1/2^+\) near a critical-line zero

\[
\zeta\left(\frac12+iE_n\right)=0.
\]

It writes

\[
\zeta\left(\frac12+iE\right)
=
Z(E)e^{-i\theta(E)}
\]

and, while assuming the target zero is simple, derives a divergent transfer amplitude whose phase can be cancelled by the observer-boundary phase.

The normalizability condition is written as

\[
\cos\!\left(
\theta(E_n)
-\pi\left(n+\frac12\operatorname{sign}n\right)
+\vartheta
\right)
=1.
\]

For positive zeros this gives the source's equation

\[
\frac{\vartheta(E_n)}{\pi}
=
n+\frac12-\frac{\theta(E_n)}{\pi}
=
n-\langle\mathcal N(E_n)\rangle+\frac32.
\]

The source explicitly states:

> to hear a given zero one has to fine tune \(\vartheta\)

according to this relation.

Therefore the exact point-spectrum construction presented in the paper assigns a boundary phase \(\vartheta(E_n)\) that depends on the target zero.

## 6. Fixed-domain consequence

This distinction is decisive for the protected Hilbert–Pólya contract.

A self-adjoint extension parameter is part of the operator domain. If the boundary phase is changed from one target energy \(E_n\) to another, the construction has changed the self-adjoint realization.

The paper observes that the phases are numerically concentrated near \(0\) and that, if fluctuations were absent, one phase could serve all average zeros. But the exact source construction does not prove that a single constant \(\vartheta\) makes all Riemann ordinates discrete eigenvalues of one self-adjoint Hamiltonian.

Thus the audited source establishes:

\[
\text{independent prime mechanism}
\quad+\quad
\text{target-dependent point-state tuning},
\]

not

\[
\text{one fixed self-adjoint operator with all raw ordinates as point spectrum}.
\]

This is a source-semantic contract failure, not a proof that no repaired Rindler model can exist.

## 7. Further evidentiary limitations in the source

Several additional limitations prevent promotion of the paper's RH-like conclusion.

### 7.1 Simple-zero assumption

The critical-line expansion explicitly assumes that the zero \(E_n\) is simple. Simplicity of every nontrivial zeta zero is not known.

### 7.2 Formal Perron/residue formula

For finite partial sums of the Möbius Dirichlet series, the source writes residue expansions and states that the equations are “formally exact” and that it would be interesting to prove them and determine their validity range.

A formal identity with unsettled validity cannot carry an RH proof.

### 7.3 Neglected terms in the point-state argument

The source first derives the point-state behavior after temporarily neglecting the additional zero-residue summands, then argues they should be subleading for critical-line zeros.

This is heuristic/asymptotic evidence, not an exact all-zero spectral theorem.

### 7.4 Weak-coupling and infinite-array limit

The construction of the zero states is made in the limit of infinitesimal reflection amplitudes and an infinite mirror array. Exact operator convergence, domain stability, and spectral convergence through this limit are material proof obligations if the limit is to support an exact Hilbert–Pólya claim.

### 7.5 Off-critical-zero contradiction

The source argues that an off-critical zero would introduce a term growing like a positive power of the mirror cutoff and would appear to destroy normalizability for every energy. It then notes the paradox with self-adjointness and says this “seems to provide a proof” of RH.

The paper immediately adds that one must be very cautious because the result relies on several unproven assumptions; its conclusion states that more work is required to support the claim.

Therefore this passage is not admissible as an RH proof result.

## 9. False-proof firewall

Reject the following moves.

1. **Prime-input success to RH success.** Encoding \(\mu(n)\) or \(\log p\) is not yet a zero-to-eigenvalue theorem.
2. **Per-zero domain tuning.** A boundary phase \(\vartheta(E_n)\) chosen separately for each target zero does not define one fixed self-adjoint operator.
3. **Average-phase substitution.** Numerical concentration of \(\vartheta(E_n)\) near zero does not prove one exact constant phase works for all zeros.
4. **Conditional reciprocal-zeta circularity.** A formula used in \(1/2<\sigma<1\) under an RH assumption cannot support a later proof of RH.
5. **Simple-zero promotion.** An argument assuming every target zero is simple cannot establish the general RH statement.
6. **Formal-residue promotion.** A formally derived Perron/residue expansion whose range is unsettled is not proof-quality spectral control.
7. **Limit substitution.** A weak-coupling/infinite-array limiting heuristic is not automatically a self-adjoint spectral theorem for the limiting operator.
8. **Self-adjointness paradox.** A heuristic contradiction between an off-line zero contribution and normalizability cannot be used to conclude RH until every limiting and residue step is rigorous.
