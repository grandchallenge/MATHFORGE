# OPENMATH-2026 failure risks

The sprint has a short wall-clock horizon, which increases pressure to collapse epistemic distinctions. The following failures are prohibited.

1. **Statement drift.** Solving an adjacent, weakened, strengthened, or differently quantified theorem while presenting it as the hill.
2. **Archive substitution.** Treating an UnsolvedMath/Ulam summary as the competition statement when the AutoLab hill differs.
3. **Stale-open assumption.** Repeating an "open" label without a dated literature/status audit.
4. **Machine-check conflation.** Treating prover acceptance as evidence that the encoded theorem faithfully matches the advertised mathematics.
5. **Cosmetic fragmentation.** Splitting one mathematical fact into many nominal claims merely to increase submission count.
6. **Duplicate-agent convergence.** Sending the full hill to many agents with the same framing instead of decomposing independent obligations.
7. **Failure erasure.** Discarding counterexamples, blocked routes, missing hypotheses, or formalization obstacles that should improve subsequent attempts.
8. **Competition-pressure overclaim.** Allowing leaderboard or deadline incentives to alter the claim boundary.
9. **Unattributed tool dependence.** Omitting material AI, CAS, solver, search, or library dependencies from provenance.
10. **Certification leakage.** Allowing Forge or Solve integration, CI success, or an external checker to stand in for MATHCERT where GCL certification is claimed.

The preferred response to uncertainty is a narrower explicit statement, not a stronger implied one.
